04 / Reflection & Collaboration

Growing through group work.

A personal reflection on teamwork, communication and shared responsibility, guided by Bruce Tuckman's five stages of group development.

My reflection focus

Understanding how people come together, work through differences, establish routines and learn from a shared task.

The experience

Learning to work as one team

Group work has helped me understand that completing a task with other people requires more than simply dividing the work. It also requires listening, patience, accountability and respect for different opinions. As a student, I am learning that every member contributes something valuable, and that a group becomes more effective when its members understand their responsibilities and support one another.

Tuckman's model helped me think about how a group can develop over time. The five stages are Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing and Adjourning. Although these stages are a useful guide, I understand that groups do not always move through them in a perfect order.

01

Stage One

Forming

At the beginning of group work, members need time to get to know one another, understand the task and establish what is expected. I recognise that people can have different personalities, levels of confidence and ways of approaching an assignment.

My responsibility at this stage is to be approachable, participate in discussions and listen carefully. I also need to understand the group's goal before concentrating on my individual contribution.

02

Stage Two

Storming

During group work, differences in opinions, ideas and working styles can create challenges. Some members may prefer to start immediately, while others may want more time to plan. I have learned that different approaches do not automatically mean that people are unwilling to cooperate.

If disagreements arise, I should remain calm, listen to the reasons behind each opinion and focus on what will help the group achieve its objective. I also need to express my views respectfully instead of assuming that my approach is always the best one.

03

Stage Three

Norming

As a group becomes more organised, members begin to establish shared expectations. This can include agreeing on deadlines, dividing tasks, communicating progress and deciding how to bring everyone's work together.

I can contribute by being reliable, respecting agreed deadlines and keeping other members informed about my progress. I understand that clear responsibilities make it easier for everyone to contribute fairly and reduce unnecessary confusion.

04

Stage Four

Performing

In the performing stage, the group works more confidently towards its goal. Members understand their roles and can concentrate on completing the task rather than repeatedly discussing how to organise themselves.

I believe I can support this stage by completing my responsibilities, helping where possible and remaining open to feedback. I also recognise the importance of checking the final work together so that the group's submission is clear, consistent and complete.

05

Stage Five

Adjourning

Adjourning takes place when the group finishes its task and brings its work to a close. This stage is an opportunity to consider what went well, what could have been improved and what each member learned from the experience.

For me, finishing a group assignment should not only mean submitting the work. It should also mean reflecting on my contribution, appreciating the efforts of others and identifying skills I want to develop further. I can use these lessons in future assignments, presentations and workplace situations.

Personal development

What I take away from teamwork

Communication matters

I have learned that instructions and expectations should be discussed clearly. Asking questions and listening carefully can prevent misunderstandings and help everyone stay focused.

Responsibility builds trust

Each member should take ownership of their assigned work. I want to continue building a reputation as someone who can be relied on and who respects the time and effort of others.

Differences can be useful

People bring different ideas and strengths to a team. Instead of dismissing opinions that differ from mine, I should consider whether they can improve the final result.

Reflection supports growth

Looking back at a group task helps me recognise what I did well and where I need to improve. This is important as I develop the professional skills required in business and data analytics.

Final thoughts

The kind of teammate I want to be.

This reflection has helped me understand that successful teamwork depends on the contribution of every member. I want to be a team member who communicates respectfully, takes responsibility, remains open to feedback and helps create an environment where others can contribute their ideas.

As I continue studying Business Intelligence and Data Analytics, these skills will be important in academic projects and in my future career. Business and data-related work often involves collaborating with people who have different responsibilities and perspectives. Learning how to work effectively with others now can prepare me for those professional situations.

“Good teamwork is not about doing everything alone; it is about making a meaningful contribution to a shared goal.”
